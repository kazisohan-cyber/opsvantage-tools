How to prioritise CVEs with CISA KEV and EPSS

Why CVSS alone is a poor patch order, what CISA KEV and FIRST EPSS add, a simple ranking rule you can defend to management, and how to apply it to a vulnerability scan.

Updated 2026-11-07 · 7 min read

The scan finished overnight: 1,800 findings, 400 of them rated High or Critical. Nobody can patch 400 things this week, and patching them in CVSS order means spending days on vulnerabilities nobody exploits while the one that ransomware crews are using sits in the queue.

Prioritising CVEs with CISA KEV and EPSS fixes most of that. Both are free, both are updated constantly, and together they answer the question CVSS can't: is anyone actually exploiting this?

Why isn't CVSS enough?

CVSS measures how bad a vulnerability would be if exploited: network reachable, no authentication, full compromise. It says nothing about whether exploitation is happening. A large share of published CVEs score 7.0 or above, but only a small fraction are ever exploited in the wild. Sorting by CVSS gives you a long list of equally urgent-looking items and no way to choose.

What is CISA KEV?

The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue is CISA's list of CVEs with reliable evidence of exploitation in real attacks. It's short (around 1,500 entries, against hundreds of thousands of CVEs), each entry has a required action and a due date for US federal agencies, and CISA marks the ones known to be used in ransomware campaigns.

If a CVE in your environment is on KEV, it's no longer a question of if. Those go first.

What is EPSS?

The Exploit Prediction Scoring System, from FIRST, gives each CVE a probability of exploitation activity in the next 30 days, based on a model fed with real exploitation data. It updates daily. A score of 0.15 means a 15% chance; most CVEs sit well below 1%.

EPSS is the early warning. A vulnerability can jump from 0.5% to 40% within days of a public exploit, long before it lands on KEV.

What's a sensible ranking rule?

PriorityRuleTypical target
Patch nowOn CISA KEVDays, not weeks
HighEPSS 10% or more, or CVSS 9+ with EPSS at least 1%Within the next patch cycle
MediumCVSS 7+ with little exploitation signalNormal monthly patching
LowEverything elseBundle with routine updates

The thresholds are judgement calls, and you can tune them. What matters is that the rule is written down, so when someone asks why a CVSS 9.8 waited while a 7.5 got patched first, the answer is "the 7.5 is being exploited and the 9.8 isn't".

Where does exposure fit in?

KEV and EPSS describe the vulnerability, not your network. Two adjustments matter most:

  • Internet-facing systems move up. A Medium on a VPN gateway, firewall or web server is more urgent than a High on an isolated build server. Most KEV entries are edge devices for a reason.
  • Compensating controls move things down. If the vulnerable service is disabled, or the device sits behind something that blocks the attack path, record that and schedule the patch normally.

How do I apply this to a scan export?

  1. Export the findings with their CVE IDs (Nessus, Qualys, Defender Vulnerability Management and OpenVAS all can).
  2. De-duplicate the CVEs: the same one on 300 hosts is one decision.
  3. Look up KEV status and EPSS for each, and rank with the rule above.
  4. Join the ranking back to the hosts, and bump internet-facing ones.
  5. Re-run monthly. EPSS changes, and KEV grows by several entries a week.

For KEV entries on systems you can't patch quickly, check for signs of compromise too. Several KEV vulnerabilities, Citrix Bleed among them, let attackers steal sessions that survive the patch. The new admin account guide covers one of the first things to look for.

FAQ

How often is EPSS updated?

Daily. Scores can change sharply when exploit code is published or attacks are observed.

Is KEV only relevant to US government agencies?

No. The due dates are binding for US federal agencies, but the list itself is the best free source of "exploited in the wild" for anyone.

What if a CVE has no CVSS score yet?

New CVEs are sometimes published before scoring. Treat a KEV or high-EPSS entry as urgent regardless, and check the vendor advisory for severity.

Should I still patch Low priority CVEs?

Yes, just not first. Most get fixed by routine cumulative updates without separate effort.
